Most of the Bay Area’s data centers sit in Santa Clara, at the heart of Silicon Valley.

One of the region’s newest is planned somewhere else: Pittsburg, a city of about 76,000 on the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta.

It’s about an hour’s drive from Carlmont when the traffic is light.

In May 2018, Pittsburg’s City Council voted to close the city-owned Delta View Golf Course for good. A month later, it agreed to start planning a “technology park” on the land.

The plan covers 76 acres of the old course. The Contra Costa Canal cuts it in two.

The data center would go on the 22 acres north of the canal. The 54 acres to the south are set aside for future phases.

City and state filings describe what’s planned: a three-story data center, an electrical substation, and a yard of 37 diesel generators, 3 megawatts each, along the building’s west side.

Today the land is empty. PG&E transmission towers line its east edge.

The nearest backyard fences are about 85 feet from the property line.

The final city approval came Dec. 11, 2025, at a hearing before a single official. Most people in Pittsburg say they did not hear about it until this spring.
