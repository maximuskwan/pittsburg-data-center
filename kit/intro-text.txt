Data centers — the warehouses of servers behind every search, stream and AI chatbot — are spreading across the country.

In 2023, they used about 4.4% of all U.S. electricity. By 2028, that share could reach 6.7% to 12%, according to a 2024 report from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

As of October 2026, 1,599 more data centers are on the way: 400 are under construction and another 1,199 are planned, according to Data Center Map.

California is home to 289 operating data centers, according to Data Center Map, most packed into Silicon Valley.

California voters will decide Nov. 3 whether to approve Proposition 45, which would speed environmental review for projects it defines as “essential” and has become a flashpoint over whether data centers could qualify.

Most of the Bay Area’s data centers sit in Santa Clara, at the heart of Silicon Valley.

One of the region’s newest is planned somewhere else: Pittsburg, a city of about 76,000 on the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta.

It’s about an hour’s drive from Carlmont when the traffic is light.

AI is already part of the school day there. After a pilot in the 2025-26 school year, the Sequoia Union High School District adopted MagicSchool, an AI platform for teachers and students. Every request to a tool like it is answered by servers in a data center somewhere.

In May 2018, Pittsburg’s City Council voted to close the city-owned Delta View Golf Course for good. A month later, it agreed to start planning a “technology park” on the land.

The plan covers 76 acres of the old course. The Contra Costa Canal cuts it in two.

The data center would go on the 22 acres north of the canal. The 54 acres to the south are set aside for future phases.

City and state filings describe what’s planned: a three-story data center, an electrical substation, and a yard of 37 diesel generators, 3 megawatts each, along the building’s west side.

Today the land is empty. PG&amp;E transmission towers line its east edge.

The nearest backyard fences are about 85 feet from the property line.

The final city approval came Dec. 11, 2025, at a hearing before a single official. Most people in Pittsburg say they did not hear about it until this spring.
