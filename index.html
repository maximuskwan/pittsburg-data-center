Power hungry: One Bay Area city’s fight against the AI data center boom

By Maximus Kwan · Nov. 7, 2026

Data centers are rapidly spreading across the country.

In 2023, they used about 4.4% of all U.S. electricity. By 2028, that share could reach 6.7% to 12%, according to a 2024 report from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

As of October 2026, 1,599 more data centers are on the way: 400 are under construction and another 1,199 are planned, according to Data Center Map.

Map: OpenStreetMap contributors, as of October. Counts: Data Center Map. Energy figures: Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, 2024 United States Data Center Energy Usage Report.

California is home to 289 operating data centers, according to Data Center Map, most of them packed into Silicon Valley.

Most of the Bay Area’s data centers are in Santa Clara, at the heart of Silicon Valley.

One of them is planned somewhere else: Pittsburg, a city of 76,416 on the Sacramento-San Joaquin River Delta.

The former Delta View Golf Course sits in southwestern Pittsburg, just south of Highway 4.

In May 2018, Pittsburg’s City Council voted to close the city-owned golf course for good. A month later, it agreed to start planning a “technology park” on the land.

Imagery: U.S. Geological Survey, The National Map.

The Contra Costa Canal splits the 76-acre plan area in two. Twenty-two acres will go to the north for the first phase, and 54 acres will go to the south for later ones.

Boundaries: City of Pittsburg, Notice of Preparation, Feb. 28, 2024 (traced; approximate).
