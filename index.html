Power Hungry

How a Bay Area city approved an $800 million data center on its old golf course — and why almost no one noticed.

By TK reporter name · TK publish date

Scroll ↓

Data centers — the warehouses of servers behind every search, stream and AI chatbot — are spreading across the country.

In 2023, they used about 4.4% of all U.S. electricity. By 2028, that share could reach 6.7% to 12%, according to a 2024 report from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory.

Most of the Bay Area’s data centers sit in Santa Clara, at the heart of Silicon Valley.

The region’s newest one is planned somewhere else: Pittsburg, a city of about 76,000 on the Sacramento–San Joaquin River Delta.

It’s about an hour’s drive from Carlmont.

TK: one or two sentences connecting a Carlmont student’s daily digital use to the electricity and water a data center consumes.

In May 2018, Pittsburg’s City Council voted to permanently close the city-owned Delta View Golf Course.

A month later, the council agreed to begin bringing a “technology park” to the land.

Imagery: U.S. Geological Survey, The National Map.

In November 2024, the council voted 5–0 to approve what that technology park would hold: “Perseus,” a 92-megawatt hyperscale data center from AVAIO Digital Partners, which the developer valued at $800 million.

Site boundary: Contra Costa County GIS / City of Pittsburg Vesting Tentative Map, Nov. 2024.

Most residents didn’t learn about it until June 2026 — about 19 months later.

Drone footage: Cara Esten Hurtle.

This is the story of how that happened — and whether a city that learns of a decision after it has been made can still change anything.
